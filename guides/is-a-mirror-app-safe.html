Is a Mirror App Safe? Here's What to Check Before You Install One

Handing an app your front camera is a fair thing to hesitate over. Most mirror apps are harmless; a few are ad-delivery machines with a camera view bolted on. Here's how to tell the difference in about a minute, and what Mirrorly does with your camera.

Red flags

  • Permissions that make no sense for a mirror — photo library, contacts, location, microphone. A mirror needs the camera. That's it.
  • An account or sign-up screen. There is no reason a mirror needs to know who you are.
  • "Data Used to Track You" on the App Store privacy label. That's ad-tracking SDKs riding along.
  • A "free trial" that's actually a weekly subscription. Check the price under the Download button before you tap.
  • A privacy policy that doesn't say what happens to camera frames. Silence is not reassurance.

Green flags

  • Camera permission only, requested when you open the mirror.
  • A plain statement that the camera feed stays on the device and nothing is captured, stored or uploaded.
  • No ads. No account. A one-time price, or free.

Checking on your own phone

Go to Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera to see every app with camera access and switch any of them off. You can also open Settings → Privacy & Security → App Privacy Report to see whether an app has been contacting servers it shouldn't.
